COGS vs cost of sales — what's the difference?

COGS (the big number) is the inventory-value method: last month's ending inventory, plus everything received this month including shipping cost lines, minus what's on hand now. It captures every reason stock value left: items sold, used in drinks, spoiled, wasted, or lost in a stocktake.

Cost of sales only counts stock that went out on completed sales orders, valued at Unleashed's average landed cost at the time of sale (which already includes shipping). Write-offs are stock adjustments that reduced inventory value.

The two should land close together. A gap means stock value moved without a sales order or an adjustment explaining it: usage not recorded as sales, unrecorded waste, purchase costs changed after receipt, or documents entered with an earlier date.
